# CP01: Competitive benchmark

Status: research note for the owner's Studio Production Directive 05 (creative principles: understand, decide, anticipate, celebrate, invest). It is not a design, not an implementation and not an acceptance claim. Every source was accessed on 27 November 2026. Claims are limited to what the cited page said on that day. My own reasoning is labelled **[Inference]**. Short quoted phrases are as reported from the page; player review text is paraphrased unless quoted.

Purpose: learn what long-running sports management games and cricket media do well and badly, so that Lord Cricket XXVI can build an original identity. Nothing here proposes copying another product's assets, branding, screen layouts or distinctive visual designs.

## Method and limits

- Sources: official store and developer pages, published reviews, Steam review pages (top rated, English), a developer forum, and app store listings.
- Steam review pages show only the first ten or so reviews per filter. They are a sample, not a measurement of sentiment.
- Several pages could not be read (see "Sources not accessed"). Where I relied on a search result summary rather than the page, I have not used it as evidence.
- Review scores and counts are as shown on the day and will change.

## Summary by product

### Cricket Captain (2026 and 2025 editions)

| Aspect | Finding |
|---|---|
| Strengths | Long-standing niche product with a 3D match view, commentary, a player editor and a score predictor [1][3]. The 2026 page promises a match screen "redesigned for clearer, faster gameplay" and faster contracts, saves and predictor [1][6]. |
| Retention drivers | Positive 2025 reviewers with 87 to 650 hours cite the player editor, a faster interface and a revamped T20 mode [5]. Several negative reviewers also have 138 to 400 hours [2][4]. **[Inference]** Players stay for cricket depth even while unhappy with it. |
| Presentation techniques | 3D highlights with "smoother animations" [1]; added commentary lines [1][3]; a score predictor [3]; Steam achievements listed without detail [1][3]. |
| Complaints | Limited overs batting control: aggression settings that feel ineffective, with "Full aggression is just an out button unless you get lucky" on the developer forum [7] and similar 2025 reviews [4]. Little visible change year to year and an interface judged unchanged or phone-like [2][4][5]. No clear guidance on what works or how training helps; one reviewer felt the game "actively wants to punish you" [2]. Missing information such as who captains the opposition [2]. Visual flicker [2]. Real-name licensing gaps [4][5]. |
| Lesson for us | Make every control's effect legible and testable. Explain why outcomes happened. Show visible progress between releases. |

### Football Manager 26 (and the FM27 response)

| Aspect | Finding |
|---|---|
| Strengths | Reviewers praise the match engine and tactics; Operation Sports calls it "a brilliant tactical sandbox trapped inside a messy interface" and says watching matches "is genuinely fun again" [10]. Positive Steam reviewers say "the match engine saved it" [12]. |
| Retention drivers | Tactical depth that becomes "incredibly rewarding" once understood; long saves, with one reviewer describing a cycle of a season or two then a break [12]. |
| Presentation techniques | Tile and card system; a "Portal" combining home screen and inbox with filters (All, New, Tasks, Unread), advice, fixtures and a two-week calendar; search with an in-game glossary; bookmarks [8]. Stated principles: "Efficiency, Familiarity, and Predictability" [8]. |
| Complaints | More clicks for routine tasks ("3-5x more clicks than FM24"), oversized panels, and statistics and in-match data reduced or removed [11]. Operation Sports says the UI hides scouting, transfer and club news and makes the world feel distant [10]. |
| Lesson for us | The developer's own FM27 page says it cut clicks, made cards full screen, refocused tiles on information rather than navigation, and added density settings (Default, Detailed, Condensed) [9]. Dense, predictable screens matter more than novelty to long-session players. |

### Out of the Park Baseball 27

| Aspect | Finding |
|---|---|
| Strengths | Depth and statistics; positive reviewers with 300 to 1,550 hours praise new advanced stats, roster building and scouting [16]. The developer stresses "dozens of new statistics" and reworked calendars, player pages and in-game screens [14]. |
| Retention drivers | Defector's writer describes long arcs where "every game adds up into one long narrative" and attachment to individual prospects [13]. A reviewer bought it because a family member appears in it; another recounts a championship run [16]. |
| Presentation techniques | Season-scale zoom: trends read "in weeks and months, rather than by at-bats" [13]. Robust stat tracking that the writer says works as a teaching tool [13]. |
| Complaints | UI alignment issues, "no proper focal point with too many bars and numbers", truncated text, hard menus, crashes and save corruption, and a weaker in-game 3D screen [15]. |
| Lesson for us | Long careers create stories when history is kept and surfaced. Density needs a focal point. |

### Motorsport Manager

| Aspect | Finding |
|---|---|
| Strengths | Race days are called "the likely best part" of the game [17]. |
| Retention drivers | Season objectives with job risk: miss the promised result and you can lose your job [17]. |
| Presentation techniques | Pit stop previews show expected time cost and rejoin position before you commit [17]. Drivers criticise bad calls, and "they're right, and you know they are" [17]. In the earlier mobile edition, final laps were "incredibly tense" because you cannot steer, only instruct, and late failures can change a result [18]. |
| Complaints | Major decisions can feel reactive and "a little too distant and hands-off"; a long tutorial that skips the key factor [17]. The mobile review says things "do get a little repetitive" [18]. |
| Lesson for us | Preview a decision's likely cost, then let the outcome land. Characters reacting to the player's calls create accountability. |

### F1 Manager 2024

| Aspect | Finding |
|---|---|
| Strengths | Race weekends with mechanical failures that force decisions; hundreds of new radio lines; Create-A-Team progression [19]. Smooth camera switching from track map to cockpit [20]. |
| Retention drivers | Varied career starts (budget, facilities, supplier) and a Mentality system with trade-offs [20]. |
| Presentation techniques | Broadcast-style race view with switchable cameras and replays [20]. |
| Complaints | Interface "largely identical" to the previous year [19]; information density takes adjustment and navigation has too many steps; iterative rather than transformative [20]. |
| Lesson for us | Annual sameness is noticed. Dense data needs short paths. |

### EA Sports FC 26 Career Mode

| Aspect | Finding |
|---|---|
| Strengths | Manager Live: weekly and monthly scenario challenges with rules, objectives and once-only rewards; completion screens recognise progress even when some objectives are missed [22]. |
| Retention drivers | Rotating challenges; a manager job market with sackings, poaching and applications that improve with over-achievement [22]. |
| Presentation techniques | "Unexpected Events" that pause simulation and present decisions with short-term and long-term trade-offs; their frequency can be adjusted or turned off [22]. |
| Complaints | Interface "largely the same, functional, faintly dusty"; menus and cutscenes "could use modern polish"; limited team choice in some challenges [23]. |
| Lesson for us | Interruptions are acceptable when they carry a real choice and the player controls their frequency. |

### NBA 2K26 MyGM and MyNBA

| Aspect | Finding |
|---|---|
| Strengths | Team-specific offseason scenarios with three goals tied to roster and history; season budgets and directives agreed with the owner; facility investment [24]. |
| Retention drivers | Achievements "chronicled through dynamic banners" [24]. **[Inference]** A visible, persistent record of success in the team's own space. |
| Presentation techniques | Era settings and in-world banners [24]. |
| Complaints | Heavy.com: modes "stagnant", and long-term goals feel "more like forced tasks than genuine franchise management" [25]. |

### Cricket TV broadcast graphics

| Aspect | Finding |
|---|---|
| Strengths | ICC TV's 2019 World Cup coverage used player tracking (fielder positions, distances, running speed), multi-angle annotated replays and over 100 data templates fed by CricViz [26]. |
| Retention drivers | Producers aimed to make coverage "more dynamic and appealing to younger audiences" [26]. |
| Presentation techniques | Dense top-ten lists were simplified into top-five rankings with large photos and bold text; "Different demographics perceive data sets in different ways" [26]. |
| Complaints | Not established by the sources I could read. |
| Lesson for us | Simplify a graphic to the few items that matter at that moment. Use the established cricket chart grammar (score summary, wagon wheel, pitch map, worm, Manhattan) as conventions, drawn in our own visual language. |

### Cricket analytics platforms

| Aspect | Finding |
|---|---|
| Strengths | ESPNcricinfo's Superstats (with IIT Madras): a Forecaster for projected score, win probability and next-over run and wicket probabilities; Smart Stats that weight runs and wickets by pressure and opposition quality; a Luck Index for dropped catches and umpiring errors [27][28]. CricViz offers WinViz over time, expected metrics (xW, xR) and per-ball impact [29]. Cricbuzz lists ball-by-ball commentary, a "Ball Map", partnership graphics, win probability and animations for boundaries, wickets and milestones [30]. |
| Retention drivers | Fast ball-by-ball updates and alerts: "Never miss a moment" [30]. |
| Presentation techniques | Context-weighted numbers; probability over time; milestone animation [27][29][30]. |
| Complaints | A CricViz user disliked a scrolling banner, "we have to wait until it scrolls along" [29]. |
| Lesson for us | Context makes numbers meaningful. Never hide key state behind motion. |

## Cross-cutting findings

1. **The match is the heart; menus decide whether people stay.** FM26 is praised for its engine and criticised for its menus [10][11][12]; FM27's developer response was fewer clicks and more density [9]. OOTP and F1 Manager draw similar interface complaints [15][20].
2. **Control must be legible.** Cricket Captain's most persistent complaint, across several editions, is batting controls whose effect players cannot read or trust [4][7].
3. **Explain outcomes.** A 2026 reviewer cites no guidance on what works or how training helps [2]. Context-weighted measures exist in cricket media for this reason [27][28].
4. **Preview consequences before commitment.** Motorsport Manager's pit stop preview shows cost and position before the call [17]; forecasts of score and win chance do the same in cricket [27][29].
5. **Tension comes from indirect control.** Instruct, then watch: Motorsport Manager's final laps [18] and F1 Manager's failures [19].
6. **Long careers become stories when history is kept.** OOTP players describe multi-season narratives and attachment to prospects [13][16]; NBA 2K chronicles achievements in banners [24].
7. **Achievement means more when it is earned in context.** Smart Stats value runs under pressure more highly [28]; FC 26 recognises partial progress [22]. Generic goals read as chores [25].
8. **Annual sameness is noticed.** Cricket Captain [2][4], F1 Manager [19], Pro Cycling Manager [21] and FC 26 [23] all drew "unchanged" comments.
9. **Density needs a focal point.** OOTP reviewers complain of "too many bars and numbers" [15]; broadcast producers cut lists to five [26]; FM27 offers density settings [9].
10. **Interruptions need choice and control.** FC 26 lets players tune event frequency [22]; a CricViz user disliked waiting on a scroll [29].
11. **Characters react to the player.** Drivers criticising calls in Motorsport Manager [17] and radio lines in F1 Manager [19] give decisions a human consequence.
12. **Visual upgrades do not rescue a dated management layer.** Pro Cycling Manager [21] and Cricket Captain [1][2] both upgraded visuals while reviewers focused on unchanged systems.

## Avoid

- Controls whose effect cannot be seen or tested, especially limited overs batting intent [4][7].
- Outcomes with no explanation of cause [2].
- Hiding information the player needs, such as opposition captain or match data [2][11].
- Extra clicks for routine weekly tasks; oversized panels that lower density [9][11].
- Dense screens without a focal point; truncated text [15].
- Scrolling or animated key state the player must wait for [29].
- Checklist goals unconnected to the club's situation [25].
- Interruptions without a choice or a frequency setting [22].
- Cosmetic renewal in place of system change [19][21].
- Copying any product's interface, layout, branding, assets or distinctive graphics. **[Inference]** A fictional game gains nothing from looking like a licensed one.

## Original direction for Lord Cricket XXVI

Each principle maps to a Directive 05 creative principle. All are **[Inference]** drawn from the findings above.

**Understand**
1. Every result explains itself: after a wicket, an over or a match, show the two or three causes in plain cricket language, drawing on the authoritative event record.
2. Context before totals: present runs and wickets with the pressure and opposition behind them (finding 7), in our own fictional measures.
3. One focal point per screen, with optional detail on request, and a density choice for long sessions.

**Decide**
4. Every control shows its expected effect before commitment and its observed effect afterwards, so the player can learn it (finding 2).
5. Routine weekly decisions take few steps from one predictable place; nothing the player needs is hidden.
6. Decisions that interrupt play are rare, carry a real trade-off, and have a player-set frequency.

**Anticipate**
7. Preview the cost of a call (projected total, win chance, risk of a wicket) as a range, then let the over play out ball by ball (findings 4 and 5).
8. Build the over and the chase as the unit of drama: a visible target, a running equation and a clear moment of decision.
9. Use cricket chart conventions (worm, Manhattan, wagon wheel, pitch map) drawn in Lord Cricket's own visual style, never a broadcaster's or platform's look.

**Celebrate**
10. Milestones land at the moment they happen, briefly and without blocking state; the score stays readable.
11. Characters react: players, captain and board respond to the user's calls, for good and ill.
12. Recognise partial success and performance under pressure, not only trophies.

**Invest**
13. Keep and surface history: player careers, records, rivalries and season reviews that a long save can look back on (finding 6).
14. Goals arise from the club's actual situation and story, not a generic list.
15. Each release changes something the player can feel in the systems, not only the visuals (finding 12).
