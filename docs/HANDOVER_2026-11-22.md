## 2. Mission and quality bar

The target is a studio quality cricket management game that is graphically interesting, sounds good and is fun to play, built on a simulation that stays statistical, deterministic and testable. Today the game is a strong engine with a plain interface; the experience work so far has been about making the numbers legible and the career feel like a story, not about art or audio.

**What is true now.** The simulation, career, persistence and recovery layers are mature and heavily tested. The interface is a React page styled by one stylesheet (apps/web/src/pavilion.css). The match view shows a scoreboard, ball call, over strip and text commentary. I did not hear the game's sound, and no human has playtested it, so every claim about feel in the experience brief is design judgement, not measured player behaviour.

**The bar, as testable statements.**

- A new player reaches a finished first match without reading a manual, and can say what to do next on every screen.
- Every match tells a story: a situation strip, a turning point, a report.
- Every season has a goal, a finale and a reason to start the next one.
- The game is recognisably cricket to look at (grounds, kit, light, crowd) and to listen to (bat, crowd, commentary, ambience), without breaking determinism or the tab's responsiveness.
- Nothing visual or audio is required to understand a result; sound can be switched off and text scales to 150 percent without breaking the layout.

Sections 6 and 7 turn this into work: section 6 is the committed queue, section 7 is a proposed roadmap for graphics, audio and feel that the owner has not yet approved.

## 4. Hard limits and repo rules

These come from the repo's own instruction files and the experience brief. They apply to every session, including ones that add graphics and sound.

- Branch and draft PR only. Never push to main, never merge, never change repository settings or owner gates.
- No change to simulation results. The ball-by-ball engine is deterministic and tested for it; art, audio and interface work must not alter outcomes, random draws or their order.
- No wider saved format. A new persisted field needs a new version and an owner decision. Per-viewer conveniences (a report already seen, a sound on or off choice) go in localStorage inside try and catch, never in a save.
- Projections stay pure. New match, career and season summaries are pure, deterministic, unsaved functions in packages/projections/src, derived from data that already exists.
- Evidence discipline. Record PASS, FAIL, BLOCKED or NOT RUN with the exact command and revision. Say which runs were local and which were hosted CI. Say what is implemented and what is merged.
- Run npx tsc -b after every pull, and check exit codes rather than reading the tail of the output.
- Accessibility floor. Text scales to 150 percent, everything works by keyboard, motion respects reduced motion, and no meaning is carried by colour or sound alone.
- If a permission is denied, stop and tell the owner. Do not look for another route.

## 7. Studio quality roadmap (proposal)

[Proposal] Everything in this section is my design suggestion for the owner to approve, change or reject. None of it is built, and I have not measured performance budgets or player reactions. Three tracks run after section 6 and can overlap once the first phase of each is approved. All three obey the hard limits: no change to simulation results, no wider saved format, and every effect has a text equivalent.

### Track V: Visual identity

1. V0 Art direction (owner decision). Choose a direction before any asset work: for example a warm illustrated pavilion look, or a clean broadcast graphics look. Deliver three labelled mock-ups of the match screen for the owner to pick from.
2. V1 Theme layer. Move colours, type scale, spacing and surfaces in pavilion.css into named design tokens, with light and dark themes and team colours fed from the kit editor values the game already stores.
3. V2 Match scene. A 2D scene (SVG or canvas) beside the scoreboard: ground, field placings drawn from the captain's tactics, a ball trace and wagon wheel built from the existing ball events. It reads events; it never produces them.
4. V3 Kit, emblems and people. Render the custom kits and emblems, and give players procedural portraits or silhouettes so the squad screen has faces.
5. V4 Motion and polish. Short transitions for wickets, boundaries and milestones, with a reduced motion path and a frame-time budget the owner agrees and Playwright traces confirm.

### Track S: Sound

1. S0 Audio direction (owner decision). Style, loudness target and where assets come from. Only owner-approved, licensed or self-generated assets, each recorded with its source and licence in the repo.
2. S1 Audio layer. One module in apps/web using the Web Audio API, driven by match events, with master volume, mute, and the per-viewer sound-on choice from E5. Off until the player opts in. Nothing in the simulation imports it.
3. S2 Ambience. A crowd bed that follows the E4 situation strip (quiet early in a Test, tense in a close chase), plus bat, ball and appeal sounds on the matching events.
4. S3 Interface sounds. Small cues for confirm, error and season milestones, all optional.
5. S4 Commentary. Text commentary already exists and stays the source of truth. Voiced commentary is out of scope until the owner decides on voices and licensing.

### Track F: Fun and feel

1. F1 Playtest protocol. Watch a small number of people play the first hour without help; record where they stall or stop. This is the first real evidence about fun, so schedule it as soon as E5 merges.
2. F2 Balance pass. Decide the intended scoring level per format (the T20 totals near 70 are the first question), then tune with the existing statistical distributions and record before and after results. Any simulation change needs explicit owner approval and a new determinism baseline.
3. F3 Pacing. Sim speed, skip-to-key-moment and match length controls so a season respects the player's time.
4. F4 Onboarding and progression. The first match, first season goal and first finale should chain without a manual; reward systems only if playtests ask for them.

**Gate for every phase.** A spec in Chromium and Firefox; the 150 percent text and keyboard checks; the determinism evidence unchanged; screenshots or a recording attached to the PR for the owner to approve before the next phase starts.

## 8. Verification and reporting

**Before opening or updating a PR.**

1. npx tsc -b exits 0.
2. Lint passes on the files you changed.
3. The new unit tests and the package's UI spec pass in Chromium and Firefox.
4. The neighbouring specs that touch the same screens pass (for navigation work, every career spec; for match screen work, the scenario and match specs).
5. npm run verify:09:core and npm run smoke pass, or are recorded as NOT RUN with the reason.
6. git status shows only intended files.

**Traps found while building.**

- npm run smoke rewrites docs/evidence/determinism-firefox.json. Discard that change rather than commit it, unless the task is to refresh the evidence.
- Temporary Playwright configs made for a container (a different port or browser path) must not be committed.
- A skip link or anchor that sets href="#..." can navigate away from a hash-routed screen; use a click handler that scrolls and focuses.
- Adding blocks above the primary action can push it off screen at 150 percent text on a 768 pixel high viewport; the m6g player-page spec checks this.
- Tab names are used by many specs. Renaming a tab means updating every spec that looks it up, in both tests/ui and tests/m6g.

**Report format for the owner.** For each package: what changed in plain words, the branch and commit, the exact commands run with PASS, FAIL or NOT RUN, anything not verified (marked [Unverified]), screenshots for visual work, and the single decision needed next. No recap of effort, no completion percentages, no dates you cannot support.
